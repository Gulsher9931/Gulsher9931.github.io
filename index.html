Research focus

Oversight and reliability of LLM systems

I trained in image processing and applied deep learning: sparse representations for image denoising and super-resolution, biomedical signal and image analysis, and, most recently, deep learning that stays robust when training labels are wrong. I have 15+ peer-reviewed papers and 380+ citations, including work in IEEE Signal Processing Letters and IEEE Access.

Since 2024 I have run LLMs in production for teachers and doctors. Those systems taught me that the hard part is rarely getting an answer from the model. The hard part is knowing when to trust it, and designing the human review around that. That is the empirical AI safety work I want to do next.

  • Scalable oversight
  • Human review of LLM judgments
  • LLM evaluation in high-stakes domains
  • Sycophancy under expert pushback
  • Robustness to label noise
  • Distribution shift
Selected work

What I have built, and what it showed

  • 1,946LLM gradings reviewed by teachers
    91.7%of AI grades approved

    GradexAI Paused

    LLM exam creation and grading, with a teacher reviewing every grade

    I designed LLM-generated rubrics, OCR for handwritten scripts and outcome analytics. Seventeen institutes and 1,516 users piloted it: 270 exams, 1,464 student submissions, 1,946 AI gradings.

    Open question it left me with: teachers approved 91.7% of AI grades, but we never measured how many of those approvals were wrong. Whether reviewers catch model errors, or quietly defer to them, is the question I most want to study.

  • 6,946doctor-verified MCQs in the pipeline
    93%of 316 error reports resolved automatically

    GulfMedExams Live

    Gulf medical licensing exam preparation for doctors

    LLMs restructure, quality-scan and tag recalled questions by topic and subspecialty. A two-stage deduplication step (OpenAI models plus embeddings) builds a high-yield "Repeat Vault". A scheduled AI resolver handles user error reports (189 fixed, 105 dismissed as correct).

    A deliberate safety trade-off: we kept AI internal, with no AI-generated questions and no user-facing chat, because one hallucination would cost credibility with doctors. The platform earned $12K+ in under 4 months, with 265 paid users in 40 countries; 39 of 40 surveyed users reported passing their exam.

  • 0answers generated live by an LLM

    AI Maths Tutor Shelved

    O-Level maths prototype

    Worked solutions were loaded and checked in advance instead of generated live. A student clicks the step they are stuck on and a chatbot explains that step. We shelved it before launch to focus our 4-person team on GulfMedExams.

  • 1stprize, DICE Innovation competition

    Agronomist

    Student AI flower-identification app I product-led as faculty mentor

    A model trained on clean photos fails in the field, so I had the team collect images on different phones and resolutions, from different angles, at different times of day and in different weather. It won first prize and a place in the university's startup incubator.

A project I would start with

Do overseers catch wrong LLM grades?

When a capable model grades work and a human approves it, how often is the approval a rubber stamp?

  1. Plant known errors in LLM-graded short answers, using public or synthetic student work.
  2. Measure catch rates for human reviewers and for weaker models acting as reviewers.
  3. Test interventions: showing the rubric, a model-written critique, or two models debating the grade.
