Research focus

Oversight and reliability of LLM systems

Three threads connect my earlier signal-processing work to the AI safety questions I want to study next.

01 · Scalable oversight

Do human reviewers catch model errors?

When people approve AI judgments at high rates, it is unclear whether the model is right or the reviewer is deferring. I want to measure the difference.

Evidence: GradexAI human-review loop, 1,946 gradings

02 · Reliability in high-stakes domains

Where should an LLM be allowed to answer?

In medical exam prep I kept the model behind the scenes, checking and tagging content, rather than answering doctors directly.

Evidence: GulfMedExams pipeline and error resolver

03 · Robust learning

Learning when the labels are wrong

Noisy labels in vision data and noisy preferences in human feedback are the same problem at different scales.

Evidence: label-noise framework, IEEE Access 2025

What I built, and what it showed

Numbers below come from each product's own dashboards.

GradexAI

Paused

LLM exam creation and grading, with teacher review of AI grades

Built
LLM-generated rubrics, OCR for handwritten scripts, and outcome analytics for accreditation reports.
Reach
17 institutes, 1,516 users, 270 exams, 1,464 student submissions.

GulfMedExams

Live

Gulf medical licensing exam preparation for doctors

Built
LLMs restructure, quality-scan and tag recalled questions; two-stage deduplication (OpenAI models plus embeddings) builds a high-yield "Repeat Vault".
Decision
No AI-generated questions and no user-facing chat. One hallucination would cost credibility with doctors.
Result
$14.3K+ revenue from 314 paying customers since the June 2026 launch, in 40 countries; renewals rose to 36% of purchases in October (from 16% in June–September) with no auto-renewal; 39 of 40 surveyed users reported passing.

A project I would start with

Do overseers catch wrong LLM grades?

When a capable model grades work and a human approves it, how often is the approval a rubber stamp? GradexAI gave me the setting; this project would give it a measurement.

Read the essay · Research plan & code on GitHub

  1. Plant known errorsSeed LLM-graded short answers with errors of graded difficulty, using public or synthetic student work.
  2. Measure catch ratesCompare human reviewers with weaker models acting as reviewers, across error types.
  3. Test interventionsShowing the rubric, a model-written critique, or two models debating the grade.
  4. Release itPublish the dataset, evaluation code and results openly.
